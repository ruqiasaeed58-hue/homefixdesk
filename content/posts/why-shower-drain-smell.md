A shower that smells like a sewer, rotten eggs, or mildew is unpleasant — and the cause is usually right under your feet. Shower drains collect hair, soap scum, and body oils that feed bacteria (biofilm), and the whole mess sits in a warm, damp pipe. Here is how to identify your smell and eliminate it.

## Quick diagnosis: what kind of smell?

- **Rotten egg / sulfur smell** → bacteria feeding on hair and soap scum (biofilm) in the drain. See Steps 1–2.
- **Sewer / sewage smell** → dry P-trap letting sewer gas up, or a venting problem. See Step 3.
- **Musty / mildew smell** → mold in the drain, on the shower curtain, or in grout. See Step 4.
- **Smell only when water runs** → buildup deeper in the pipe being disturbed. See Step 2.

## What you will need

- Rubber gloves
- Drain brush (long flexible type, $8–$12) or an old toothbrush
- Baking soda (1/2 cup)
- White vinegar (1 cup)
- Boiling water (kettle-full)
- Tweezers or needle-nose pliers
- Old towels

## Step 1: Remove the hair and gunk

Hair is the foundation of drain smell — it traps soap scum and gives bacteria a home.

1. **Remove the drain cover.** Most lift or unscrew; some have a single screw.
2. **Pull out everything you can reach** with gloved fingers, tweezers, or pliers. It will be disgusting. That is the smell.
3. **Scrub the drain walls** as far down as you can reach with the drain brush or toothbrush. The slimy coating on the pipe walls (biofilm) is a major odor source — mechanical scrubbing removes what chemicals cannot.
4. Replace the cover (or upgrade to a hair-catcher cover, $5–$10, to prevent recurrence).

## Step 2: The baking soda + vinegar + boiling water flush

This loosens and flushes the biofilm deeper in the pipe.

1. Pour **1/2 cup baking soda** directly into the drain.
2. Follow with **1 cup white vinegar**. It will fizz — that is the reaction working through the grime.
3. **Wait 15–20 minutes**, then flush with a full kettle of **boiling water**.
4. Run the shower hot for 2–3 minutes to rinse everything down.

For stubborn buildup, repeat once. Do not use chemical drain cleaners here — they are overkill for odor, damage pipes with repeated use, and the fumes in a small shower are nasty.

## Step 3: Refill a dry P-trap

Every drain has a U-shaped P-trap that holds water as a seal against sewer gas. In a guest bathroom or a shower that is rarely used, the water evaporates — and sewer gas comes straight up.

1. **Pour 2–3 cups of water** into the drain to refill the trap. The smell often vanishes within minutes.
2. For rarely-used drains, add a **tablespoon of mineral oil** after the water — it floats on top and slows evaporation dramatically.
3. If the smell returns quickly even with regular use, the trap may be siphoning dry from a venting problem — that needs a plumber to diagnose.

## Step 4: Attack mold sources

If the drain is clean but the shower still smells musty:

1. **Shower curtain/liner:** the #1 hidden mold source. Wash it in the machine with hot water and vinegar, or replace it ($10–$20).
2. **Grout lines:** scrub with a baking soda paste and an old toothbrush; reseal grout annually.
3. **Behind the drain cover:** mold grows on the underside of metal covers — scrub both sides.
4. **Ventilation:** run the exhaust fan during and 20 minutes after every shower. Chronic moisture is the root cause of bathroom mold.

## Keeping it fresh

- Install a hair-catcher over the drain and empty it weekly.
- Pour boiling water down the drain monthly as maintenance.
- Run the exhaust fan religiously.
- Squeegee the shower walls after use — less standing water means less biofilm.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Pouring bleach down the drain as a first resort.** Bleach kills surface bacteria but does not remove the biofilm they live in — the smell returns in days. Scrub first.
- **Ignoring a dry P-trap.** If the smell is sewer gas, no amount of cleaning fixes it — the trap needs water.
- **Using chemical drain openers for odor.** They do not remove biofilm effectively and damage pipes, septic systems, and your lungs.
- **Forgetting the shower curtain.** People deep-clean the drain while the moldy curtain hangs two feet away.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my shower smell like rotten eggs?**
Sulfate-reducing bacteria in the biofilm produce hydrogen sulfide (rotten egg gas) as they digest soap and hair. The scrub + baking soda/vinegar routine eliminates their food source.

**Is sewer gas from the drain dangerous?**
In small amounts it is mostly unpleasant, but sewer gas contains methane and hydrogen sulfide — in high concentrations these are hazardous. A persistent strong sewage smell deserves prompt attention.

**How often should I clean the shower drain?**
Remove hair weekly (or whenever you see it), do the baking soda/vinegar flush monthly, and deep-scrub quarterly.

**My whole bathroom smells, not just the drain — what else could it be?**
Check the toilet's wax ring (a failed seal lets sewer gas up around the base), the sink overflow channel (scrub it — it harbors biofilm), and the exhaust fan (a dust-choked fan does not clear moisture).

## When to call a pro

- Sewer smell persists with a full P-trap — likely a venting problem or a cracked drain pipe; a plumber's camera inspection finds it.
- Multiple drains smell — the main vent stack may be blocked (bird nest, ice, debris).
- Water drains slowly along with the smell — there is a partial blockage deeper in the line.
- You suspect a failed toilet wax ring — the toilet needs to be pulled and reset ($150–$250).
