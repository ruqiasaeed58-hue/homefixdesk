A toilet that keeps running after flushing is almost always caused by water leaking from the tank into the bowl — usually because the rubber flapper is worn out or the fill valve is set too high. The fix takes about 15 minutes and costs under $10 in parts.

## Quick check: find the leak in 2 minutes

1. Remove the tank lid and look inside. Is water flowing into the overflow tube (the tall open pipe in the middle)? If yes, the fill valve water level is too high — skip to the fill valve fix below.
2. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank water and wait 10 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and needs replacing.

## Cause 1: Worn-out flapper (most common)

The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. Over time it warps or gets coated in mineral deposits and no longer seals.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off the water supply valve behind the toilet.
2. Flush to empty the tank.
3. Unhook the old flapper from the overflow tube pegs and detach the chain.
4. Take it to the hardware store to match the size, or buy a universal flapper.
5. Install the new flapper, reconnect the chain with about half an inch of slack, turn the water back on.

## Cause 2: Fill valve set too high

If water pours into the overflow tube nonstop, the tank is overfilling.

**Fix:** Find the adjustment screw or clip on the fill valve float. Lower the float so the waterline sits about one inch below the top of the overflow tube. Flush and check.

## Cause 3: Tangled or short chain

If the chain is tangled or too short, it holds the flapper slightly open. Re-hook it so there is a little slack when the flapper is closed.

## When to call a plumber

- Water is leaking from the base of the toilet onto the floor (this may be a failed wax ring).
- The shut-off valve behind the toilet is stuck or corroded and will not turn.
- You have replaced the flapper and adjusted the fill valve and it still runs — the flush valve seat itself may be cracked.
