A bathroom sink that drains slowly is almost always clogged with hair and soap scum wrapped around the pop-up stopper or sitting in the P-trap — not deep in the pipes. You can clear it yourself in about 20 minutes with no special skills and no chemical drain cleaner.

## Why bathroom sinks clog (the hair + soap equation)

Bathroom sink clogs are chemically different from kitchen clogs:

- **Hair** provides the structure — long strands wrap around the stopper and trap walls, forming a net.
- **Soap scum** (calcium stearate, formed when soap meets hard water minerals) coats the hair net, making it waterproof and sticky.
- **Toothpaste, shaving cream, and skin oils** fill the gaps, compacting the mass into a dense plug.

This is why chemical drain cleaners fail on bathroom sinks: they cannot dissolve the hair structure, and the soap-scum coating shields the clog. Mechanical removal — pulling the hair out — is the only reliable fix. The good news: the clog is almost always within arm's reach.

## Quick diagnosis: where is the clog?

- **Water drains slowly but eventually empties** → the clog is shallow: hair on the pop-up stopper or in the visible drain. Start with Step 1.
- **Water does not drain at all** → the clog is deeper, likely in the P-trap (the U-shaped pipe under the sink). Skip to Step 3.
- **Both sinks or the tub also drain slowly** → the blockage is in a shared branch line, past what you can reach from this sink. Try a drain snake from this drain; if that fails, call a plumber.

## What you will need

- Rubber gloves
- Old toothbrush or small bottle brush
- Bucket and towels
- Channel-lock pliers or a pipe wrench
- A drain snake / hair removal tool (a $5 plastic barbed strip works for shallow clogs; a 25-foot hand auger for deeper ones)
- White vinegar and baking soda (optional, for freshening after)

## Step 1: Pull the pop-up stopper and clean it

Most bathroom sink clogs live right here.

1. Lift the stopper to the open position. Many pull straight out — give it a firm tug and twist.
2. If it does not pull out, look under the sink: find the pivot rod going into the back of the drain pipe, held by a retaining nut. Unscrew the nut, slide the rod out, and the stopper lifts free from above.
3. You will likely find a disgusting wad of hair and soap scum wrapped around it. Pull it off with gloved hands or the old toothbrush.
4. Scrub the stopper and the visible drain opening, rinse, and drop the stopper back in (reconnect the pivot rod if you removed it).

Test the drain now — this alone fixes the majority of slow bathroom sinks.

## Step 2: Snake the drain opening

If it is still slow, push a barbed plastic hair-removal tool (or a hand auger) down the drain. Work it past the bend with a gentle twisting motion, then pull it back slowly — it drags the hair clog out with it. Repeat 2–3 times. Run hot water for 30 seconds and check the flow.

## Step 3: Clean out the P-trap

If the sink still will not drain, the clog is sitting in the U-bend under the sink.

1. **Clear under the sink** and put the bucket directly beneath the P-trap. Lay towels around — water will spill.
2. **Loosen the slip nuts** on both ends of the U-shaped trap by hand (they are designed to be hand-tight). If they are stuck, use channel-lock pliers gently — these nuts are plastic and crack if muscled.
3. **Pull the trap down** and dump its contents into the bucket. This is where the real clog usually is: a compacted plug of hair, soap, and toothpaste.
4. **Clean it out** with the bottle brush, rinse it in the bucket, and check the short pipe sections on either side for buildup too.
5. **Reassemble.** Slide the trap back up, hand-tighten both slip nuts, then give each an extra quarter-turn with the pliers. Do not overtighten.
6. **Test for leaks.** Run the faucet full-blast for a minute while watching every joint with a dry hand — even a slow drip means a nut needs another small turn.

## The vinegar flush (finishing touch)

Once water flows freely, pour half a cup of baking soda down the drain, follow with half a cup of white vinegar, wait 15 minutes, then flush with very hot tap water. This does not dissolve real clogs, but it clears soap film and kills the mildew smell.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Pouring chemical drain cleaner first.** It rarely dissolves hair clogs, it can damage older metal pipes and the rubber seals in the trap, and it turns the standing water into a chemical bath you will splash when you open the trap.
- **Overtightening plastic slip nuts.** Hand-tight plus a quarter-turn. Cracked nuts leak and mean a hardware-store trip.
- **Forgetting the bucket.** The P-trap holds a cup or two of foul water. It will find your cabinet floor if you are not ready.
- **Using a plunger on a sink with an overflow hole.** Cover the overflow with a wet rag first, or the pressure just escapes there.

## Keeping it clear

- Once a month, pull the stopper and wipe off the hair before it builds into a clog.
- Run very hot water for 30 seconds after shaving or heavy toothpaste use — it melts soap scum before it hardens.
- A $3 mesh drain cover catches hair before it enters the drain. It is the cheapest plumbing insurance there is.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my bathroom sink clog but the kitchen sink is fine?**
Different debris. Bathroom sinks collect hair + soap scum + toothpaste, which mat together into a waterproof plug. Kitchen sinks clog on grease and food — a different problem with a different fix.

**Can I use a plunger on a bathroom sink?**
Yes, with a caveat: seal the overflow hole with a wet rag first, and remove the pop-up stopper so pressure reaches the clog. A few vigorous plunges often dislodge shallow clogs.

**The water drains but smells like mildew. Why?**
Biofilm — bacteria feeding on soap and skin oils coating the inside of the drain and overflow channel. The baking-soda-and-vinegar flush plus a bottle-brush scrub of the overflow opening fixes it.

**How do I know the clog is past the P-trap?**
If the trap is clean and the snake comes back empty but water still stands, the blockage is further down the branch line. A 25-foot auger fed from this drain usually reaches it; if not, it is plumber territory.

**Why does the drain gurgle when it finally empties?**
Air trapped behind the clog escapes as water forces through — the gurgle is actually a good sign that the blockage is breaking up. If gurgling persists after the drain runs clear, the vent for that branch may be partially blocked.

**Can I prevent hair clogs if I shed a lot?**
A stainless mesh drain cover ($3–$5) is the single best investment — it catches hair before it enters the drain. Empty it every few days. For heavy shedders, brush hair before washing your face so loose strands go in the trash, not the sink.

## The monthly 2-minute routine (prevents all of this)

1. **Pull the stopper** and wipe off accumulated hair — 30 seconds.
2. **Run very hot water** for 30 seconds — melts fresh soap scum before it hardens.
3. **Wipe the drain rim** with the used hand towel — removes the biofilm ring.

Do this monthly and you will never need the P-trap step again. Clogs form over weeks; interrupting the buildup monthly keeps the drain permanently clear.

## When to call a plumber

- Multiple fixtures (sink + tub, or both bathroom sinks) drain slowly — the blockage is in a shared line.
- Water backs up into the tub or shower when the sink drains — same shared-line issue.
- You have snaked 25 feet with no improvement.
- Any sign of water damage under the cabinet — swollen wood, mold, or a musty smell that does not go away.
