A stripped screw hole — where the screw spins freely without biting — is one of the most common DIY frustrations. It happens on door hinges, cabinet hardware, furniture, and deck boards. The fix is straightforward: give the screw fresh wood to bite into. Three methods cover every situation, from a quick hinge fix to a load-bearing repair.

## Quick diagnosis: how bad is it?

- **Screw spins but the hole is small** → toothpick method (5 minutes). See Fix 1.
- **Hole is enlarged or the wood is soft/crumbly** → dowel method (stronger). See Fix 2.
- **Stripped hole in particleboard, MDF, or a high-stress joint** → epoxy or threaded insert. See Fix 3.
- **Screw head is stripped (not the hole)** → different problem: use a rubber band, screw extractor, or cut a new slot. The hole itself is fine.

## What you will need

- Wood glue (yellow carpenter's glue)
- Wooden toothpicks, golf tees, or a hardwood dowel
- Utility knife or flush-cut saw
- Drill and drill bits
- Screwdriver or drill/driver
- Two-part epoxy wood filler (for Fix 3)
- Sandpaper (120-grit)

## Fix 1: The toothpick method (quick, most cases)

The classic fix — fast, strong enough for hinges, handles, and most hardware.

1. **Remove the screw** and squirt wood glue into the hole.
2. **Push in as many toothpicks as fit** — usually 2–4. Coat them in glue as you go. For larger holes, use wooden golf tees or matchsticks instead.
3. **Snap or cut them flush** with the surface using the utility knife.
4. **Let the glue dry** — 30–60 minutes minimum, overnight is best for full strength.
5. **Drive the screw back in.** It will cut fresh threads into the packed toothpicks and hold firmly.

This works because the screw is now biting into solid glued wood instead of the stripped-out void.

## Fix 2: The dowel method (stronger, bigger holes)

For holes too large for toothpicks, or repairs that need real strength (door hinges carrying weight, gate hardware).

1. **Drill out the stripped hole** to a clean diameter matching a hardwood dowel (commonly 3/8 inch).
2. **Cut a dowel plug** slightly longer than the hole is deep. Coat it generously with wood glue.
3. **Tap it in** with a hammer until seated. Wipe off excess glue.
4. **Let it cure fully** — 24 hours for maximum strength.
5. **Trim flush** with a flush-cut saw or chisel, sand smooth.
6. **Pre-drill a pilot hole** slightly smaller than the screw, then drive the screw.

A glued hardwood dowel is essentially as strong as the original wood — this is the proper repair for anything load-bearing.

## Fix 3: Epoxy or threaded inserts (particleboard, MDF, high stress)

Toothpicks do not hold well in particleboard or MDF — the material crumbles around them.

1. **Two-part epoxy wood filler:** mix per instructions, pack it firmly into the hole, let it cure fully (usually 24 hours), then pre-drill and drive the screw. Epoxy bonds to the surrounding material and gives the screw something solid to bite.
2. **Threaded inserts:** for hardware that gets removed repeatedly (knock-down furniture, access panels), drill the hole to size and screw in a brass threaded insert with a hex key. The machine screw then threads into metal — it will never strip again.
3. **T-nuts:** for through-holes, a T-nut on the back side gives a permanent metal thread.

## Preventing stripped holes

- **Pre-drill pilot holes.** The #1 prevention. The pilot bit should be roughly the diameter of the screw's core (not the threads).
- **Use the right driver bit.** A Phillips bit in a Phillips screw, fully seated. Cam-out (the bit slipping) is what strips both heads and holes.
- **Drive screws straight.** Angled driving wallows out the hole.
- **Do not overdrive.** Stop when the screw is snug — sinking it deeper just chews up the wood.
- **Wax or soap on the threads** for hardwoods — reduces driving friction dramatically.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using a bigger screw as the "fix."** It works once, then strips a bigger hole. Fix the hole properly instead.
- **Skipping the glue.** Dry toothpicks without glue pull out under load. The glue is what makes it permanent.
- **Not waiting for the glue to cure.** Driving the screw into wet glue pushes the filler out of the hole. Wait.
- **Using softwood dowels for hardwood repairs.** Match or exceed the original wood's hardness.
- **Filling with wood filler alone (the soft kind).** Regular wood filler is for cosmetic dents, not for holding screws — it crumbles under thread pressure. Use epoxy filler if going the filler route.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does the toothpick fix last?**
Years, in most cases — the glued toothpicks become part of the wood. For hinges that get daily stress, the dowel method lasts longer.

**Can I fix a stripped hole in a metal door frame?**
Different approach: use a larger self-tapping screw, a rivet nut, or fill with epoxy and re-tap. Wood methods do not apply to metal.

**Why do my screws keep stripping?**
Usually no pilot hole, wrong bit size, or a worn driver bit. Replace driver bits regularly — a fresh bit grips dramatically better.

**Will wood glue alone hold a screw?**
No — glue fills the void but has no structure for threads to bite. You need the toothpick/dowel/epoxy filler material.

## When to call a pro

- The stripped hole is in a structural connection (deck ledger, stair railing post) — a contractor should verify the repair meets code.
- The wood around the hole is rotted — the rot needs to be cut out and the member possibly replaced, not just filled.
- It is a stripped hole in an expensive piece of furniture — a furniture repair specialist can make it invisible.
- Hinge screws keep stripping in a heavy exterior door — the door may need re-hanging with proper long screws into the framing (a carpenter job, $100–$200).
