An occasional clog happens to everyone. But a toilet that clogs every week has an underlying problem — and it is rarely "bad luck." The usual suspects: a weak flush that cannot clear the bowl, a partial blockage lodged in the trap, or non-flushable items going down. Work through the causes in order.

## Quick diagnosis: what kind of clogging?

- **Clogs with normal use, water rises high then slowly drains** → partial blockage in the trap or drain. See Steps 1–2.
- **Weak, lazy flush that barely swirls** → low water level or clogged rim jets. See Step 3.
- **Clogs only when too much paper is used** → could be normal, or a low-flow toilet with a weak flush. See Step 4.
- **Multiple fixtures clogging (tub, sink too)** → main drain line issue, not the toilet. See "when to call a pro."

## What you will need

- Flange plunger (the one with the fold-out rubber cup — not a sink plunger)
- Toilet auger (closet auger), 3–6 ft
- Rubber gloves
- Bucket and old towels
- White vinegar
- Toilet brush

## Step 1: Plunge correctly

Most people plunge wrong — they use a sink plunger and pump frantically.

1. **Use a flange plunger.** The fold-out flange seals the toilet's drain opening; a flat sink plunger cannot seal it and does almost nothing.
2. Make sure there is enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger cup.
3. **Push down slowly, pull up sharply.** The pull creates the suction that dislodges clogs. Gentle push (to avoid splashing), sharp pull, repeat 10–15 times.
4. Flush and watch: a strong swirl that drains fast means success.

## Step 2: Use a toilet auger

If plunging fails, the blockage is deeper — a toy, a wad of "flushable" wipes, or buildup in the trap.

1. **Extend the auger's cable** into the bowl, guiding the curved end into the drain opening.
2. **Crank the handle clockwise** while pushing gently. You will feel resistance when you hit the clog.
3. **Work through it** — crank and push until the resistance gives way, then retract the cable slowly.
4. Flush to test. Repeat once if needed.

Never use a chemical drain cleaner in a toilet — it can crack the porcelain with heat, damage the wax ring, and splash caustic liquid.

## Step 3: Fix a weak flush

A toilet that cannot flush properly will clog on normal waste. Common causes:

1. **Low tank water level.** The water should sit about 1 inch below the overflow tube. Adjust the fill valve float up to raise it.
2. **Clogged rim jets.** The small holes under the bowl rim deliver the swirl. Mineral deposits block them. Scrub under the rim with a toilet brush and vinegar; poke each jet clear with a piece of wire or an Allen key.
3. **Clogged siphon jet.** The larger hole at the bottom front of the bowl drives the main flush. Clear it the same way.
4. **Flapper closing too fast.** If the flapper drops before the tank empties, the flush is short. Adjust the chain (1/2 inch of slack) or replace an old waterlogged flapper ($5–$10).

After these fixes, test with a few sheets of toilet paper — it should vanish with authority.

## Step 4: Stop flushing the wrong things

The recurring-clog culprit in many homes:

- **"Flushable" wipes** — they do not break down like toilet paper. This is the #1 cause of repeat clogs and sewer backups. Never flush them.
- **Paper towels, tissues, cotton balls** — designed not to disintegrate.
- **Feminine hygiene products** — expand and snag in pipes.
- **Dental floss** — wraps around everything and catches debris.
- **Cat litter** (even "flushable" kinds) — clumps and hardens in pipes.

Toilet paper only. If your household uses a lot of paper per flush, flush twice — it is cheaper than a plumber.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using chemical drain cleaner in the toilet.** It rarely works on toilet clogs, risks cracking porcelain, and creates a chemical hazard for the next person who plunges.
- **Flushing "flushable" wipes.** The label is marketing. Wastewater agencies universally say they cause blockages.
- **Ignoring a gradually weakening flush.** It means buildup is growing. Fix it now before it becomes a full blockage at the worst moment.
- **Plunging with a sink plunger.** Get the flange type — it is the difference between success and a mess.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my toilet clog when I barely use any paper?**
Likely a weak flush (Step 3) or a partial blockage already in the trap (Step 2). The toilet is not clearing what a healthy flush should handle.

**Is it normal for a low-flow toilet to clog more?**
Early 1.6-gallon models (1990s) had weak flushes and clogged often. Modern low-flow toilets (1.28 gpf with pressure-assist or improved bowl design) flush as well as old 3.5-gallon models. If yours is an old weak-flusher, replacement may be the real fix.

**Can a full septic tank cause toilet clogs?**
Yes — if you are on septic and multiple drains are slow, the tank may need pumping ($300–$600). Do not ignore this; a backed-up septic system is a health hazard.

**How do I prevent clogs?**
Toilet paper only, fix weak flushes promptly, and keep a flange plunger and auger on hand. Teach the household what not to flush.

## When to call a pro

- Multiple fixtures are slow or backing up — the main sewer line may be blocked (roots, collapse). A plumber with a camera inspection ($200–$400) diagnoses it.
- The auger will not clear it — the blockage may be in the drain line beyond the toilet's reach.
- Sewage backs up into tubs or showers when you flush — main line blockage, urgent.
- The toilet rocks or leaks at the base — the wax ring has failed; the toilet needs to be pulled and reset ($150–$250).
- An old, chronically weak-flushing toilet — a modern replacement pays for itself in fewer headaches (and less water).
