A faint buzz from a light switch is common — but it is never something to ignore outright, because the same symptom ranges from "harmless dimmer hum" to "loose wire arcing inside the wall." This guide helps you tell the difference and fix the benign causes.

## Quick diagnosis: what kind of buzz?

- **Buzz only when dimmed (dimmer switch)** → almost always a dimmer/bulb compatibility issue. See Step 1.
- **Buzz at full brightness on a dimmer** → wrong dimmer type or overloaded dimmer. See Step 1.
- **Buzz from a regular (non-dimmer) switch** → loose wire connection — potentially dangerous. See Step 2.
- **Buzz plus warmth, crackling, or a burning smell** → stop using it and call an electrician now. See the warning below.

## When buzzing is dangerous

Call an electrician immediately (and stop using the switch) if you notice ANY of these:

- The switch or cover plate feels **warm or hot**
- **Crackling, popping, or sizzling** sounds (not a steady hum)
- A **burning or ozone smell**
- **Flickering** lights on the circuit
- Visible **scorch marks** or discoloration on the switch or plate

These indicate arcing — electricity jumping across a bad connection — which is a genuine fire risk. Do not "wait and see" on these symptoms.

## Step 1: Fix dimmer buzz (the common, benign cause)

Dimmer switches work by rapidly chopping the electrical current, which makes both the dimmer's internal components and some bulb filaments vibrate — the buzz you hear.

1. **Check bulb compatibility.** Old dimmers were designed for incandescent bulbs. LED and CFL bulbs need a dimmer rated for them — look for "LED compatible" or "CL/LED" on the dimmer. Mismatched combinations buzz loudly.
2. **Check the dimmer's wattage rating.** Dimmers have maximum (and minimum) loads. A 600W dimmer driving a single 9W LED can buzz because the load is below the dimmer's minimum. Modern LED-rated dimmers handle low loads properly.
3. **Try a different bulb brand.** Even "dimmable" LEDs vary — some buzz on certain dimmers while others are silent. Buy one test bulb before replacing them all.
4. **Replace the dimmer.** A modern LED-compatible dimmer ($20–$35) fixes most dimmer buzz permanently. Turn off the breaker, swap the wires one-for-one, and restore power.

A very faint hum from a dimmer at low brightness is normal physics — if you can only hear it with your ear near the switch, it is not a problem.

## Step 2: Check for loose wiring (non-dimmer switches)

A standard on/off switch should be silent. Buzzing means a connection issue.

1. **Turn off the breaker** for the circuit. Verify with a non-contact voltage tester ($15 — essential tool).
2. **Remove the cover plate and switch.** Look for:
   - Wires pushed into **backstab connections** (the push-in holes on the back) — these loosen over time and are the #1 cause of buzzing standard switches. Move the wire to the screw terminal instead and tighten firmly.
   - **Loose screw terminals** — tighten them.
   - **Nicked or damaged wire insulation** — if copper is exposed or damaged, the wire may need trimming back to clean copper.
   - **Aluminum wiring** (dull silver instead of copper orange, common in 1960s–70s homes) — aluminum connections need special handling (see below).
3. **Reinstall, restore power, and listen.** A properly wired standard switch is silent.

## Step 3: Aluminum wiring (special case)

If your home has aluminum branch wiring:

- Aluminum expands and contracts more than copper, loosening connections over time — buzzing switches are a classic symptom.
- **Do not just tighten and forget.** Aluminum-to-device connections need antioxidant paste and CO/ALR-rated devices, or professional pigtailing with copper (the CPSC-recommended repair).
- If you find aluminum wiring and are not experienced with it, this is electrician territory — improper aluminum connections are a documented fire cause.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Ignoring a hot or crackling switch.** The benign causes are silent or faintly humming; heat and crackling are never benign.
- **Working without killing the breaker.** And verify with a tester — mislabeled breakers are common.
- **Leaving backstab connections in place.** If you open a buzzing switch and find backstabs, move them to screws while you are in there — it is a 2-minute upgrade that prevents future problems.
- **Mixing bulb and dimmer types blindly.** "Dimmable LED" + "old dimmer" is the most common buzz combination in American homes.
- **Overloading a dimmer.** Add up the bulb wattages — exceeding the dimmer's rating causes buzzing, overheating, and early failure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a slightly buzzing dimmer dangerous?**
A faint, steady hum at dimmed levels with no heat is normal and not dangerous. Loud buzzing, buzzing at full brightness, heat, or crackling are not normal — investigate.

**Why does only one of my dimmers buzz?**
Different bulb/dimmer combinations, different loads, or that particular dimmer is older or failing. Swap a bulb from a quiet dimmer as a test.

**Can a buzzing switch cause a fire?**
A loose connection arcing inside the box can — that is why heat, crackling, and smell are red flags. A faint dimmer hum with no heat is not an arcing risk.

**Should I replace all my old dimmers?**
If they buzz with LED bulbs, yes — modern LED-rated dimmers are $20–$35, install in 15 minutes, and eliminate both the buzz and the flicker.

## When to call a pro

- Heat, crackling, burning smell, or scorch marks — electrician, promptly.
- Aluminum wiring — needs proper CO/ALR devices or copper pigtailing.
- Buzzing from inside the wall (not at the switch) — could be a failing connection at a junction box.
- Multiple switches buzzing on the same circuit — may indicate a panel or circuit-level issue.
- You are not comfortable working with household wiring — a $150 service call is cheap insurance against a wiring mistake.
